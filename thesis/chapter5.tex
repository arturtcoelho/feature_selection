\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}

This chapter synthesizes findings from the experimental investigation, draws practical conclusions, and identifies directions for future work.

\section{Summary of Findings}

This thesis evaluated SHAP-based feature selection for tabular regression through a two-stage protocol. Stage~1 screened four selection strategies across four datasets and three model families. Stage~2 performed focused analysis of SHAP-intensive methods using XGBoost on two datasets.

The central finding is that SHAP-based feature selection does not provide consistent advantages over simpler alternatives in the settings studied:

\textbf{Predictive effectiveness}: SHAP achieved the best RMSE in only 2 of 12 dataset-model configurations in Stage~1, both with XGBoost. In Stage~2, custom SHAP recursive elimination occasionally outperformed native importance at intermediate retention levels, but gains were marginal, typically less than 1\%. In many configurations, the full-feature baseline matched or exceeded all selection methods.

\textbf{Computational cost}: SHAP-based strategies involve additional computation compared to native importance, particularly for bagging ensembles where TreeExplainer is less efficient. The hybrid strategy adds complexity without corresponding accuracy gains.

\textbf{Selection stability}: SHAP-based methods showed somewhat lower stability at aggressive retention levels ($k=0.25$) in Stage~1, though this pattern was not dramatic. Stability concerns do not strongly differentiate SHAP from alternatives.

\section{Interpretation}

These results do not suggest SHAP is without value; it remains powerful for model explanation~\cite{lundberg2020local}. However, the evidence does not support treating SHAP importance as a superior selection criterion for general regression practice, consistent with recent findings~\cite{wang2024feature}.

The lack of consistent SHAP advantage likely reflects:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Redundancy with native importance}: For tree-based models, SHAP values and native importance often correlate strongly, so additional SHAP computation may not surface different rankings.
    \item \textbf{Model robustness}: Modern ensemble methods are robust to irrelevant features, reducing the benefit of explicit selection.
    \item \textbf{Dataset characteristics}: The datasets studied may not exhibit conditions where fine-grained SHAP rankings matter.
\end{itemize}

\section{Practical Recommendations}

Based on the experimental evidence:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Start simple}: Begin with a full-feature baseline. Many datasets do not benefit from explicit selection.
    \item \textbf{Use low-cost methods first}: Mutual information or native importance rankings provide competitive results at minimal cost.
    \item \textbf{Reserve SHAP for targeted use}: SHAP-based selection may be justified when there is specific evidence it helps, or when interpretability is the primary goal.
    \item \textbf{Avoid expensive hybrids without validation}: Complex strategies do not automatically outperform simpler methods. Justify their cost with demonstrated gains.
    \item \textbf{Consider the full trade-off}: A method achieving marginally lower RMSE but requiring significantly more time may not be preferable in practice.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scope}: Four datasets and three model families cannot represent all regression problems. Results may differ on time-series data or high-dimensional settings.
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameters}: Models used default or lightly-tuned configurations. Joint optimization might reveal different patterns.
    \item \textbf{Strategy implementations}: The custom SHAP-RFE and hybrid approaches are specific implementations; alternatives might perform differently.
    \item \textbf{Metrics}: The study focused on RMSE, stability, and runtime. Interpretability or regulatory requirements might favor different choices.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work}

\textbf{Broader coverage}: Evaluating on additional domains (healthcare, finance, industrial sensors) would test generalizability.

\textbf{Alternative architectures}: Extending to neural tabular models (TabNet, NODE) or other boosting implementations (LightGBM, CatBoost) would assess transferability.

\textbf{Joint optimization}: Nested hyperparameter tuning where selection parameters are optimized alongside model parameters could reveal missed interactions.

\textbf{Cost-aware selection}: Adaptive strategies that trigger SHAP computation only when ranking uncertainty is high might reduce overhead while preserving benefits.

In closing, this thesis asked whether SHAP-based feature selection justifies its computational cost in tabular regression. The evidence suggests SHAP provides at best marginal advantages over simpler alternatives. For practitioners under computational constraints, simpler methods remain the pragmatic default. SHAP's value lies primarily in its explanatory power, not as a selection criterion.
